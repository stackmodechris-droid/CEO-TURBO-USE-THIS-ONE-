Salon & Barbershop Outreach Playbook

One daily routine: walk in, send personalized emails, follow up on a schedule, track every touch. Every number below is a limit or a goal you can measure, not a promise of sales.

1. The answer first

15walk-ins per day (best channel)
20personalized emails per day, max
0cold texts. Text only people who opted in
35total touches per day
  • Cold emails: 20 a day. Personal, one specific line about their shop. Above about 25 a day from a new address, Gmail and Outlook start sending them to spam. Keep one sending address per 20-a-day.
  • Cold SMS: 0 a day. Texting a business's cell phone without consent is covered by the US TCPA, and a business line doesn't exempt it. Carriers also block unregistered marketing texts (your SMS campaign already got flagged once for this). Use text only after they reply, ask for info, or hand you their number to text. See section 6.
  • Walk-ins: 15 a day, 2 hours max. Walk in, ask for the owner or lead barber, show the card on your phone or a real card, ask one question. You close here. Email and walk-ins go together: email first, then walk in the same week.
Ads: keep the Google search ad off for now. You're paying for 20-30 clicks a day and getting zero sales. That means the page, the offer or the follow-up isn't converting, and more clicks won't fix it. Turn ads back on only after the walk-in and email routine produces at least 2 booked calls or 1 card sale from the same audience. Then restart with a small budget aimed at the new /salons or /barbershops pages, not the general page.

5. Walk-in script (in person)

Walk in during a quiet time. Ask for the owner or lead stylist. If they're busy, ask for a good time to come back and leave the card. Keep it under 90 seconds.

"Hi, I'm {{Your name}}, I help local salons get more 5-star Google reviews. Quick question: when a client leaves happy, what do you do to get them to review?" [Let them answer. Then:] "We put a card on the station. The client taps it with their phone and your Google review page opens. Takes ten seconds. Can I leave one for you to try at your chair? No charge." [If they say yes, leave a card and your flyer with the booking link. If they're interested in the full plan:] "I can do a free 30-minute call and look at your Google profile with you. Want me to put you on the calendar this week?"

Leave the card, not a long pitch. If they say no, thank them and leave. Log it.

6. Text messages (only with consent)

Texting is allowed when the person has done one of these:

  • Replied to your email or asked you to text them
  • Gave you their number and said it's OK to text (written or spoken, and you note the date)
  • Booked or asked about a call with you
{{Your name}} from CEO Turbo. You asked for a 2-minute look at your Google profile. Want it? Reply YES or STOP to opt out. Msg & data rates may apply.

Every text needs your business name, what they signed up for, how often you'll text, and STOP/HELP. Your SMS terms and privacy page already cover this on the site. Never text a number you bought, scraped, or pulled from a directory.

8. Rules that keep you out of trouble

  • Every email includes your name, a real physical postal address, and a working unsubscribe. Fill in {{Physical postal address}} before you send anything.
  • Honor every unsubscribe and every STOP the same day. Move them to Do not contact.
  • Never write a subject line that looks like a reply, a notice or a missed message.
  • Never promise a result (more bookings, a rank, a number of reviews). Say what you'll set up, then do it.
  • Never offer a discount, free service or gift for a review. Google bans it and can remove the reviews.
  • Don't claim a salon or barbershop is a client or has used the cards unless they have. Use real names and real permission only.
  • If someone says they're already set up with a marketing company, thank them and stop. Don't argue.

9. Tracker columns

Use docs/outreach-tracker.csv. Columns: date, business, type (salon/barber), city, contact name, phone, email, personal detail used, channel (email/walk-in/text), step (A/B/C/D or walk-in), outcome (sent/replied/booked/card sold/no/closed/do not contact), next follow-up date, notes.
